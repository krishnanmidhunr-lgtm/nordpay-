Expense policy

A practical employee expense policy: five things to make clear

6 min read · Team operations

An expense policy should help people make routine decisions without waiting for finance to answer every question. Keep it short, easy to find and specific enough to apply consistently.

Start with these five points

  • Eligible expenses: explain what the company reimburses and what needs advance approval.
  • Limits: set clear thresholds by category, trip or role, and say who can approve exceptions.
  • Receipts: state what proof is needed and what to do when a receipt is missing.
  • Timing: give a submission deadline and explain when reimbursements are processed.
  • Ownership: show who submits, reviews and approves each expense, and where to ask for help.

Review the policy when your team, travel patterns or tools change. Ask employees and approvers which rules are hard to interpret, then update the lines that create repeated questions. A clear policy is a working guide, not a document people only find during onboarding.

Workflow

From receipt to reimbursement: where the process loses time

5 min read · Process design

A slow reimbursement process is often a chain of small waits: a missing receipt, an unclear category, an approval sent to the wrong person, or a payment batch prepared only once a month.

Map the handoffs

Write down each step from purchase to payment. Note its owner, the information needed and how long it typically waits. Look for repeat rework: employees asked to resubmit details, managers unsure what they are approving, or finance correcting coding at the end.

Fix the cause first

Make required fields clear, route requests using agreed approval rules, and show approvers the amount, purpose and receipt together. Set a regular reimbursement cadence and tell employees what to expect. Track turnaround time and the share of submissions returned for changes to see whether the process is improving.

Finance visibility

Expense reporting that helps you act, not just look back

5 min read · Reporting

A useful expense review connects numbers to decisions. Follow a few stable measures month to month and investigate meaningful changes instead of starting with a long list of transactions.

A focused monthly check

  • Spend by category and team, compared with budget or prior periods.
  • Time from submission to approval and from approval to reimbursement.
  • Submissions returned for missing details, receipts or policy clarification.
  • Exceptions that repeat and may need clearer guidance or approval rules.

Treat unusual values as a prompt to ask questions, not proof of a problem. Confirm timing, team changes and accounting treatment before drawing conclusions. Finish each review with one or two actions, an owner and a follow-up date. That turns reporting into a useful feedback loop.
